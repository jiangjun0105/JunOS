# Bottom-Up

Looking back over what I've written here, the same shape keeps appearing. I didn't set out to
have a philosophy; it just turns out that everything I trust was built the same way.

## The harness

When I built my [agent harness](/article/agent-harness), I refused to start from an org chart.
Defining a "Product Manager agent" and a "Tech Lead agent" is easy; knowing whether they can
actually get work done is not. So I automated nothing until I had run it by hand and watched it
work. Skills first, verified one at a time, then a facilitator on top, then issues, then epics,
then whole projects. Each layer sits on a layer already proven. Automation only triggers a skill
that already works.

The frameworks I looked at and didn't adopt all shared the opposite instinct: start with the
top-level concept — digital employees, agent teams — and hope the foundation holds. Top-down
solutions without a proven foundation.

## The brain

Evolution built brains the same way I built the harness. Starting hundreds of millions of years
ago from simple structures, life kept reusing the simple things that already worked and extending
them: a working model, then scale. Emotion itself is a bottom-up product — a paramecium's
approach-or-avoid reflex, elaborated across a billion years into jealousy.

And the fly connectome taught me that what evolution produced is robust because of its
[structure, not its parameters](/article/structure-over-weights). We do the opposite in deep
learning: hand-design a fixed architecture top-down, then train weights inside it. I think future
training will have to grow structure the way biology did, from a body outward.

## Learning

When I learn, I ask. Each answer has to attach to something I already understand, extending the
existing frame by one block. A course drops knowledge onto a blank sheet without knowing what I
already know, and those islands vanish. Knowledge that grows on the existing skeleton stays,
because it is connected to everything around it. That is
[bottom-up learning](/thoughts/learning-by-asking): from the working model I already have, outward.

## The body

Even the gym taught me this. You don't fix a body by making one muscle big. You get the whole
chain — foot, ankle, knee, pelvis, spine — assembled the way it was designed, then add core
stability. A flat foot at the bottom of the chain can end as back pain at the top.
[Fix the foundation](/thoughts/the-body-is-one-system).

## The rule

Start from something that works. Extend it one verified step at a time. Never build the top floor
first and hope the ground holds. Nature had no choice but to work this way; I've found I get
better results when I don't give myself the choice either.
