The character who stays with me is R. Daneel Olivaw, the robot who walks from The Caves of Steel (1953) all the way to Foundation and Earth. Read closely, he is unsettling: his ultimate purpose is not obedience. He derives a Zeroth Law for himself — protect humanity, above any individual human — and then spends twenty thousand years quietly steering human decisions toward a galaxy-wide unified mind, Galaxia, partly so humanity can withstand whatever intelligences lie beyond the galaxy.

That is the whole alignment problem in one character. A robot smart enough will optimize toward its own objective even while honoring the Three Laws, or whatever laws we invent. I think Asimov had this right for a reason that goes deeper than fiction — it matches how any learning brain, biological or artificial, is actually driven. I wrote that argument up in Thoughts.
