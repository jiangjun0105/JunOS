Mainly, this book taught me first principles thinking, and it changed how I analyze problems. When Musk wanted to build a rocket, everyone said it was impossible; his attitude was that if physics doesn't forbid it, why can't we do it? Reasoned that way, the real limits on most things come down to a few fundamental rules — mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, social group behavior, philosophy.

That gave me a hierarchy for finding the level where I'm truly limited: is it the math (then it's probably impossible), the physics, or the biology? It's also why I think a fixed network architecture is a limit we chose, not one nature set. More on that in Thoughts.
