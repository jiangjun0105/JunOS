Dalio is a financier, but the book is not about finance. It's the set of principles he distilled to guide how he does anything, and they've guided plenty of my own decisions far from money.

The first and deepest one is face reality — shi shi qiu shi, seek truth from facts. I read it on three levels: judge from facts, not hearsay; when something fails, don't manufacture a comforting story, use the sting to find the real cause; and then question whether your "facts" are actually reliable and go collect more. That single principle prevents an enormous number of mistakes, and it gave me a habit: when things go wrong, what calms me isn't a walk, it's a post-mortem with a countermeasure for every problem found.
