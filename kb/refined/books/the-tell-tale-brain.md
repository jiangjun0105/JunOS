A neuroscientist at UC San Diego explores how the brain works using simple, almost homemade experiments — most famously relieving phantom-limb pain with a mirror box that shows the patient a reflection of their intact hand where the missing one would be. Electron microscopes exist, but he works the way physicists always have: form a hypothesis, then design a cheap, clever experiment that reveals the mechanism indirectly.

That habit of mind is the reason I recommend it. It taught me that the deepest mysteries yield to good experimental design, and it changed how I read AI papers — intuition first, then logic. The longer version of what this book did to my thinking is in the research section.
