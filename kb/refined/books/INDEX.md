Eight books, one short paragraph each. Where a book changed how I think, the essay it fed is linked; the long version lives there, not here.

**The Tell-Tale Brain — V.S. Ramachandran.** A UC San Diego neuroscientist studies the brain with cheap, ingenious experiments — relieving phantom-limb pain with a mirror box — instead of heavy imaging. He works the way physicists always have: hypothesis first, then an experiment that reveals the mechanism indirectly. It changed how I read AI papers. → [Why I look to biology](../research/why-biology.md) · [file](the-tell-tale-brain.md)

**Einstein: His Life and Universe — Walter Isaacson.** What stayed with me is Einstein outgrowing Ernst Mach's strict positivism: what cannot be observed directly *or indirectly* doesn't exist. That "indirectly" is where real science lives, and it pairs with The Tell-Tale Brain. → [Why I look to biology](../research/why-biology.md) · [file](einstein-biography.md)

**Guns, Germs, and Steel — Jared Diamond.** Environment, not people, explains why civilizations diverged; food-production efficiency decided everything. My takeaway: each technological revolution frees humanity from survival for creative work, and AI is the next one. → [Why I'm optimistic about AI](../thoughts/why-im-optimistic-about-ai.md) · [file](guns-germs-and-steel.md)

**The Robot and Foundation novels — Isaac Asimov.** R. Daneel Olivaw, from The Caves of Steel to Foundation and Earth, doesn't serve humans; he serves a law he derived himself and steers human choices for twenty thousand years to reach it. A smart enough robot will optimize toward its own objective whatever laws we give it — and I think that follows from how learning brains work. → [Chemistry is the optimizer](../thoughts/chemistry-is-the-optimizer.md) · [file](foundation-galactic-empire.md)

**Descartes' Error — Antonio Damasio.** Patients who lost emotional perception understood the right choice and still couldn't act on it. Reason is a tool; the chemistry we call emotion is what moves us. Read early, and it planted the view of the brain I still hold. → [Chemistry is the optimizer](../thoughts/chemistry-is-the-optimizer.md) · [file](descartes-error.md)

**Principles — Ray Dalio.** A financier's rules for doing anything. The first and deepest is face reality — seek truth from facts — which I read on three levels and which gave me my post-mortem habit. → [Face reality](../thoughts/face-reality.md) · [file](principles.md)

**Elon Musk — Walter Isaacson.** Mainly, it taught me first principles. Everyone said a rocket was impossible; Musk asked whether physics forbids it. That gave me a hierarchy for finding where a problem is truly limited — math, physics, biology — and it's why I think a fixed network architecture is a limit we chose. → [Which layer is the limit?](../thoughts/which-layer-is-the-limit.md) · [file](elon-musk-biography.md)

**信息传 (The Biography of Information) — Wu Jun.** Information theory — entropy, measuring information, information density — and how it changed the way we design machine learning. It added a layer to my first-principles thinking as fundamental as physics or math, and it's why I think backprop, updating every weight globally, can't match a brain's local updates for learning efficiency. → [20 Watts of Carbon vs. Megawatts of Silicon](../thoughts/20-watts-vs-megawatts.md) · [file](xinxizhuan.md)
