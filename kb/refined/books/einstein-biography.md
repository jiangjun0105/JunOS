The part of this biography that stayed with me is philosophical, not physical. Ernst Mach shaped the young Einstein, then couldn't accept relativity; Einstein's reply — does the moon exist only when you look at it? — marks the moment he outgrew strict positivism. The lesson I took: what cannot be observed directly or indirectly doesn't exist, and that "indirectly" is where real science lives.

Read it for the reminder that the great theories began as a mental model built from imagination, then confirmed by experiments designed around that model. It pairs well with The Tell-Tale Brain.
